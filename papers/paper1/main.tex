\begin{abstract}
We introduce a nerve protocol --- discrete neuroletters
(\emph{glossary: tokens from a fixed 64-element alphabet}) drawn
from a learned local vocabulary, multiplexed on $\gamma / \theta$
rhythms (\emph{glossary: frequency bands from predictive-coding
biology associated with prediction and error signals, used here
as a structural correctness contract --- see \S~Method}), and
routed over a sparse learned topology --- that lets heterogeneous
neural modules (World Model Languages, or WMLs) \emph{exchange
information} without sharing a substrate. We validate two claims
empirically. \textbf{(A) Substrate-agnostic polymorphism.} Three
structurally distinct substrates --- stateless MLP, spiking LIF
with surrogate-gradient training, and attention-based Transformer ---
reach comparable task accuracies on a non-linear benchmark
(HardFlowProxyTask, XOR-on-noise 12-class). A four-point scaling law
traces the pairwise MLP / LIF gap from median $10.7\%$ at $N=2$
through $6.7\%$ at $N=16$, $2.4\%$ at $N=32$, plateau $2.7\%$ at
$N=64$; direction stable (LIF $\geq$ MLP) in 19/20 multi-seed
measurements (5 seeds $\times$ 4 $N$-points, with $N=2$ completed
in v1.7.0; 4/5 at $N=2$, 5/5 at $N{=}16$, $N{=}32$, $N{=}64$). The triple-substrate version closes from $8.2\%$ at
$N=15$ to $4.3\%$ at $N=60$ over 5 seeds each.
\textbf{(B) Substrate-agnostic information transmission.} For two
independently trained substrates on the same input, the mutual
information between their emitted codes over the 64-code alphabet
is $\mathrm{MI} / H = 0.91$ at $N=1$, strengthening to $0.96$ at
$N=16$ pool scale (192 cross-pair measurements) --- on post-VQ
discrete codes; the pre-VQ continuous value is $\approx 0.10$
(debiased, see \S\ref{sec:info-tx} and ADR-0001). Round-trip fidelity
through learned transducers MLP $\to$ LIF $\to$ MLP retains $99\%$
of direct-MLP accuracy; cross-substrate merge (a frozen LIF decoding
MLP-emitted codes alone through a learned transducer) retains
$97\%$. Through the \emph{full deployable discrete protocol} --- a
learned per-edge codebook transducer and a $\gamma/\theta$
phase-amplitude-coupling channel --- round-trip fidelity is $0.924$
of direct accuracy (20 seeds), recovering the idealised continuous-logit
path to within the discretisation cost. The finding generalizes to a
structurally independent distribution (MoonsTask, $\mathrm{MI} / H =
0.74$). A post-hoc analysis via Huh et al.'s~\cite{huh2024platonic} mutual-kNN kernel on continuous pre-VQ embeddings yields cross-substrate alignment $0.174$ at $k{=}10$ ($18.8\times$ the random baseline, 3 seeds), situating nerve-wml as an empirical validation of the Platonic Representation Hypothesis on heterogeneous biologically-inspired substrates. All numeric claims
are reproducible via \texttt{uv run pytest} with explicit seeds;
code and figures of this v1.7.0 snapshot are archived on Zenodo
(concept DOI \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19656342}{10.5281/zenodo.19656342},
version DOI \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19666405}{10.5281/zenodo.19666405}),
and pre-registered on OSF as part of the dreamOfkiki research
programme (\href{https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/Q6JYN}{10.17605/OSF.IO/Q6JYN}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

The nerve protocol sits at the intersection of six research lines. We relate each briefly below.

\textbf{Predictive coding.} Rao and Ballard~\cite{rao1999predictive} introduced predictive coding as a unifying interpretation of extra-classical receptive-field effects in the visual cortex, where each layer transmits its prediction downstream and receives a residual error upstream. Bastos et al.~\cite{bastos2012canonical} then proposed the canonical microcircuit for predictive coding, mapping predictions to $\gamma$-band activity in superficial layers and errors to $\theta$/$\alpha$-band activity in deep layers. The framing has since been refined: Bastos et
al.~\cite{bastos2020routing} reframe hierarchical message passing as
\emph{predictive routing} --- a layer- and rhythm-specific mechanism in
which top-down predictions in deep-layer $\alpha$/$\beta$ rhythms gate
the $\gamma$-band feedforward channel, so that errors propagate only
when predictions fail, with Gabhart, Xiong \& Bastos~\cite{gabhart2025predictive} subsequently
framing predictive coding itself as a cognitive process organised around such rhythm-specific routing.
The closest computational instantiation
is the laminar neural-mass Comparator of Ruffini et
al.~\cite{ruffini2025comparator}, which realises cross-frequency
$\gamma$/$\theta$ coupling as a laminar Comparator for prediction error evaluation, the substrate of predictive routing in the Bastos et al.~\cite{bastos2020routing} sense.
At the population level, Kim et al.~\cite{kim2026crossfreqbursts} show that coupled
spiking populations self-organise multi-frequency burst patterns that define distinct
information-routing patterns between populations, a mechanistic precedent for
frequency-multiplexed routing between modules.
Our $\gamma$/$\theta$ multiplexing (see \S\ref{sec:method}) is a
computational transcription of this predictive-routing view rather than
of the 2012 microcircuit alone: $\gamma$-priority is the engineering
analogue of the rhythm-specific gating that routes prediction and error
onto distinct channels.

\textbf{Discrete latent codebooks.} van den Oord et al.~\cite{vandenoord2017neural} introduced VQ-VAE, the vector-quantized variational auto-encoder in which a continuous encoder output is snapped to one of $K$ discrete codes. We reuse the commitment-loss training recipe for each WML's local codebook (\S\ref{sec:method}). Zeghidour et al.~\cite{zeghidour2022soundstream} observed that random-init VQ training collapses onto a small subset of codes, and proposed codebook rotation --- re-seeding unused codes with live input points. Our Plan 4a experiments confirm that rotation is necessary for random-init convergence on the $64$-code alphabet (\S\ref{sec:experiments}). Liu et al.~\cite{liu2021dvnc} further demonstrated VQ bottlenecks as inter-module communication channels (DVNC), validating that discretisation improves compositional generalisation when neural modules recombine. More recently, Peng et al.~\cite{peng2025gridlikevq} introduce Grid-like Code Quantization to compress observation-action sequences in world models, drawing on grid-cell attractor dynamics as a biologically-motivated VQ prior. Zhao et al.~\cite{zhao2025channelawarevq} propose channel-aware VQ for semantic communication on discrete channels, framing VQ as a joint source-channel coding problem. Our nerve-wml is orthogonal: we treat the codebook as a fixed protocol alphabet and measure the information-theoretic alignment of substrates emitting into this alphabet, rather than optimising the codebook for a specific communication channel or world-model prior.

\textbf{Surrogate-gradient spiking neural networks.} Neftci et al.~\cite{neftci2019surrogate} survey surrogate-gradient training of SNNs, where the non-differentiable Heaviside spike is replaced at back-propagation time by a smooth pseudo-derivative (we use the fast-sigmoid variant). This makes our \texttt{LifWML} implementation directly trainable alongside the differentiable \texttt{MlpWML} through the same task loss, which is the precondition for our polymorphie claim. At the systems level, Liu et al.~\cite{liu2024hybrid} survey \emph{hybrid neural networks} (HNN) --- the fusion of artificial- and spiking-network lineages --- and propose a qualitative taxonomy distinguishing \emph{tight} from \emph{weak} coupling. Their reference systems co-train a single hybrid network on dedicated neuromorphic hardware; nerve-wml instead occupies the \emph{weak-coupling} corner of that taxonomy, keeping each substrate independently trained and sharing only a discrete-code protocol, and it renders the resulting interface falsifiable through formal invariants (N-1--N-5, W-1--W-4) and a measured shared-information criterion rather than leaving the integration qualitative. Spike-native predictive coding is progressing in parallel: PCL+~\cite{ndri2026pclplus} learns delayed recurrent excitatory connections with spike timing-dependent plasticity for unsupervised sequence prediction, and Difference Predictive Coding~\cite{karlsson2026diffpc} trains spiking networks with sparse ternary messages, the closest published analogue to neuroletters on a spiking substrate. Both operate inside a single network; the nerve protocol standardises the message between independently trained modules.

\textbf{Representation similarity and cross-architecture communication.} Kornblith et al.~\cite{kornblith2019similarity} introduced linear and RBF Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA), a Hilbert--Schmidt Independence Criterion-based metric that is invariant to orthogonal transformation and isotropic scaling of hidden representations. Morcos, Raghu, and Bengio~\cite{morcos2018insights} introduced Projection-Weighted CCA (PWCCA) on the same broad question. Both operate on \emph{continuous} hidden states. Moschella et al.~\cite{moschella2022relative} subsequently proposed \emph{relative representations}: encoding each sample as a vector of cosine similarities to a fixed anchor set, which achieves invariance to angle-preserving transformations and enables zero-shot model stitching across independently trained models (their own Table~5 reports, for example, a ViT$\to$ResNet50 stitch accuracy of $4.69\%$ absolute vs $84.46\%$ relative on CIFAR-100). \emph{We state plainly that the existence of cross-architecture representational convergence is not novel to this work:} Moschella et al.\ and the subsequent zero-shot stitching literature established by 2022--2023 that independently trained networks converge to latent spaces differing only by an angle-preserving transform, with stitching achievable \emph{without} retraining. Our contribution is therefore not the observation of convergence per se, but its measurement at the \emph{discrete protocol-output} level across structurally heterogeneous biologically-inspired substrates (\S\ref{sec:info-tx}, \S\ref{sec:contribution-delim}). Their Figure~7 mentions vector-quantised anchors as an alternative similarity function but explicitly leaves the discrete variant as future work. Saxe et al.~\cite{saxe2024universality} argue empirically for a representation-universality hypothesis across biological and artificial networks using representational dissimilarity analysis (Pearson correlation of RDMs), also on continuous representations. Huh et al.~\cite{huh2024platonic} generalise these findings into the \emph{Platonic Representation Hypothesis}: representations across architectures, modalities, and training objectives converge toward a shared statistical model of reality. Our mutual-kNN alignment measurement in \S\ref{sec:info-tx}~(6) situates nerve-wml as an empirical validation of PRH on \emph{structurally heterogeneous biologically-inspired substrates} --- ANN vs spiking LIF with surrogate-gradient --- rather than the vision/language cross-modality pairs that dominate their evidence. We use \emph{biologically-inspired} rather than \emph{biological} to distinguish surrogate-gradient LIF from actual neural recordings, consistent with~\cite{aristotelianprh2026}'s caution against rhetorical overextension of PRH. Our measured $0.174$ sits near the lower bound of Huh et al.'s cross-modality range ($0.15$--$0.25$), which extends up to $\sim 0.50$ for same-modality pairs; this placement reflects the structural heterogeneity between ANN and spiking substrates, not a ceiling on PRH validity.

Our \texttt{MI}/\texttt{H} ratio on emitted discrete codes (\S\ref{sec:info-tx}) occupies the slot this family leaves open: we measure, at the discrete protocol-output level, what Kornblith's CKA, Morcos's PWCCA, Moschella's relative representations, and Saxe's RDMs measure on continuous hidden states. Empirically we observe $\mathrm{MI}/\mathrm{H} \approx 0.953$ versus $\mathrm{CKA}$ on the corresponding argmax one-hots of $0.910$ (\S\ref{sec:info-tx}); the $4.3$-percentage-point gap tracks soft many-to-one code mappings that kernel-alignment metrics miss by construction. Shang et al.~\cite{shang2026displacement} offer a reading of the gap between our post-VQ and pre-VQ figures (\S\ref{sec:info-tx}, Test~(1)): across independently trained encoders, the displacement vectors between samples are largely preserved under a single orthogonal map while absolute positions are not, so pointwise similarity can be weak while shared relational structure is strong. Quantisation onto a common alphabet plausibly retains the latter and discards the former; we have not tested this directly.

\textbf{Adjacent abstraction layers: model IRs and event transports.}
Two recent lines define communication structure for brain-inspired
systems at abstraction layers that bracket the nerve protocol, and we
position against both to forestall a category error. The Neuromorphic
Intermediate Representation~\cite{pedersen2024nir} is a
\emph{static computational-graph IR} --- a hardware-agnostic instruction
set that lets a single spiking model be compiled onto Loihi, SpiNNaker,
or a simulator without rewriting; it standardises \emph{what graph} a
substrate runs. The nerve protocol operates one layer above: it is a
\emph{runtime} communication contract between already-instantiated,
independently trained modules, with learned per-edge transducers and
typed $(\pi, \varepsilon)$ roles, and it makes no claim about the
internal graph of any WML.\footnote{NIR v1.0.8 (July 2026,
\url{https://github.com/neuromorphs/NIR/releases/tag/v1.0.8}) adds HDF5
compression, graph validation, and an intermediate representation for
data in SNNs, but still no inter-module message primitive; the
distinction drawn here is unchanged.} Conversely, Address-Event Representation and
its recent UDP-over-Ethernet biohybrid formalisation~\cite{aer2025biohybrid}
specify a \emph{transport and encoding} layer --- how individual spike
events are timestamped, addressed, and physically carried between a
silicon and a biological population. The nerve protocol is
substrate-agnostic precisely because it does not fix a transport: a
neuroletter is a typed, phase-tagged symbol whose \emph{semantics} are
learned, and AER (or any other event bus) could serve as its physical
carrier. NIR governs the model, AER governs the wire; nerve-wml
governs the learned message in between, and is orthogonal to both by
construction rather than by competition.

\textbf{Knowledge distillation.} Hinton et al.~\cite{hinton2015distilling} proposed training a student network end-to-end by minimising the weighted loss $\alpha\,\mathrm{CE}(p_S(\cdot; T), p_T(\cdot; T))\,T^2 + (1-\alpha)\,\mathrm{CE}_{\mathrm{hard}}(y)$, where $T$ is a temperature that softens the teacher's class distribution. Our cross-substrate merge (Test (3), \S\ref{sec:info-tx}) is structurally different: both substrates are \emph{frozen}, only a linear transducer between their emission spaces is trained, supervision is ground-truth labels only, and the passed message is a logit vector over a $64$-code protocol alphabet rather than a softmax over $n_{\mathrm{classes}}$ label values. This separates protocol channel capacity from student learning capacity.

\textbf{Delimiting the contribution.}\label{sec:contribution-delim} To pre-empt the
prior-art objection, we separate what is established from what is new. \emph{Established
(not claimed here):} (i) that independently trained networks converge representationally
across architectures and modalities, including without retraining --- this is the relative-
representations / stitching result~\cite{moschella2022relative} and the Platonic
Representation Hypothesis~\cite{huh2024platonic}; (ii) that this convergence can be
\emph{measured} on continuous hidden states by CKA~\cite{kornblith2019similarity},
PWCCA~\cite{morcos2018insights}, RDMs~\cite{saxe2024universality}, or mutual-kNN kernels.
\emph{New here:} (a) the measurement is taken at the \emph{discrete protocol-output}
level --- entropy-normalised MI on emitted $64$-code tokens, the slot the continuous
metrics leave open (\S\ref{sec:info-tx}, with a $4.3$pp CKA gap that kernel alignment
misses by construction); (b) the substrate pair is deliberately \emph{structurally
heterogeneous and biologically-inspired} (stateless MLP vs surrogate-gradient spiking LIF,
later vs attention Transformer), not the vision/language pairs dominating prior evidence;
(c) the integration of the six threads above into a single substrate-agnostic protocol
with an explicit discrete-code measurement methodology. The nerve protocol contributes
(a)--(c), not the underlying convergence phenomenon nor any individual technique.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Neuroletters}
A neuroletter is a tuple $(code, role, phase, src, dst, t)$ with
$code \in \{0, \dots, 63\}$, $role \in \{\pi, \varepsilon\}$,
$phase \in \{\gamma, \theta\}$, and $(src, dst)$ pointing at WMLs.
Each WML owns a local codebook of 64 embeddings; per-edge
transducers map between neighbours' codebooks.

\subsection{Sparse routing and priority}
The topology is a learned directed graph with $K \ll N$ active
out-edges per WML, sampled via Gumbel-softmax top-K during training
and frozen to $\{0, 1\}$ at inference. The $\gamma / \theta$ multiplex
invariant (N-3: $\text{role}{=}\varepsilon \Leftrightarrow
\text{phase}{=}\theta$) functions as a \emph{formal correctness
contract} --- a type-checker for letter well-formedness at
composition time --- rather than a differential computational
mechanism. In the experiments reported in this paper all substrates emit $\pi$
letters in the $\gamma$ phase exclusively (\S Threats to Validity), so
the invariant is satisfied by construction; its value is catching
composition bugs when downstream modules inadvertently emit malformed
letters (\S\,(7) guard-injection test). The structural inspiration is
the predictive-routing account of Bastos et
al.~\cite{bastos2020routing} and the 2025 cognitive-process framing by Gabhart et al.~\cite{gabhart2025predictive}, in
which prediction and error occupy rhythm-specific channels; the earlier
canonical-microcircuit formulation~\cite{bastos2012canonical} is the
precursor of that account. We cite this lineage as design inspiration,
not as a claim of empirical neural mechanism --- the N-3 invariant is a
formal correctness contract, and the Ruffini et
al.~\cite{ruffini2025comparator} Comparator circuit is named as the
closest biophysical model should a future version seek a mechanistic
grounding.

\subsection{WML polymorphism}
\texttt{MlpWML} implements the WML protocol via a 4-layer MLP and
a surprise-gated $\varepsilon$ head; \texttt{LifWML} implements the
same protocol via a population of LIF neurons with surrogate-gradient
spiking. Both expose only the \texttt{step(nerve, t)} entry point.

\subsection{Matched-capacity design}
\label{sec:matched-capacity}
Throughout the paper we fix the substrate capacity to
$d_{\mathrm{hidden}} = n_{\mathrm{neurons}}$ (default $16$, with
sensitivity sweep at $\{8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256\}$ reported in
\S\,(11) and Sleep-EDF matched-scale runs). This \emph{under-parameterises}
the substrates by SOTA standards (e.g.\ SLA-MLP on Sleep-EDF uses
$\sim 10^6$ parameters and achieves $95.5\%$; our $d_{\mathrm{hidden}}
= 128$ configuration uses $\sim 10^4$ parameters and achieves
$\sim 80\%$). The choice is deliberate: fixing the parameter budget
equal between MLP and LIF isolates the \emph{architectural} difference
(dense ReLU vs spiking membrane dynamics) from capacity-driven
differences. Any observed substrate-intrinsic gap (the $2$--$3\%$
plateau of Claim A) is then attributable to substrate structure, not
to one architecture having more free parameters than the other. This
rigour comes at a cost: our absolute accuracies on real-world
benchmarks are below the unrestricted-capacity SOTA. We accept that
cost in exchange for apples-to-apples substrate comparability, and
explicitly flag it in the experiments that follow. The paper's claims
are about \emph{substrate-relative} information transmission, not
about maximum achievable task competence.

\section{Threats to Validity}

Our experiments use synthetic tasks and small WML pools. Three threats deserve mention.

\textit{Task saturation and honest harder-task measurement.} \texttt{FlowProxyTask} 4-class is trivially linearly separable; both MLP and LIF pools converge to 1.0 on it. The 0\% polymorphie gap (Figure~\ref{fig:w2-histogram}) is therefore a degenerate best case. We implemented \texttt{HardFlowProxyTask} --- a 12-class variant where the class label XORs two hyperplane projections on the noise component, so a linear probe plateaus at $\sim 0.6$.

\textbf{v0.3 measurement (cosine decoder).} With \texttt{LifWML}'s original fixed cosine-similarity decoder, \texttt{run\_w2\_hard} reported $\text{acc}_{\text{MLP}} = 0.547$, $\text{acc}_{\text{LIF}} = 0.480$, a polymorphie gap of $12.1\%$. We initially attributed this to a genuine substrate limitation.

\textbf{v0.4 update (symmetric heads).} A re-read of the code revealed that the observed gap was \emph{not} substrate-intrinsic but a crippled-readout artefact: \texttt{MlpWML} used a learned linear $\pi$ head while \texttt{LifWML} used a fixed cosine match against a randomly initialized codebook. After adding a symmetric \texttt{emit\_head\_pi = nn.Linear(n\_neurons, alphabet\_size)} to \texttt{LifWML} (protocol \texttt{step()} keeps the cosine pattern-match decoder intact, preserving the N-1 invariant), the same pilot now reports $\text{acc}_{\text{MLP}} = 0.547$, $\text{acc}_{\text{LIF}} = 0.611$ --- a $10.7\%$ gap in the \emph{opposite} direction. The spike + surrogate pipeline, once given a trainable readout, edges out the $16$-dim MLP core on XOR-on-noise.

\textbf{v0.5 update (multi-seed at scale).} Scaling the hard task to a $16$-WML pool (eight MLPs, eight LIFs) gave a \emph{single-seed} gap of $1.68\%$, which we initially read as statistical closure. Multi-seed re-measurement with $\text{seeds} \in \{0,1,2,3,4\}$ reveals the true N=16 distribution: $\text{mean} = 6.29\%$, $\text{median} = 6.71\%$, $\text{p25} = 4.67\%$, $\text{p75} = 8.02\%$, $\text{max} = 10.35\%$. The seed-0 estimate was lucky; the N=16 gap is distributionally $\sim 7\%$ --- above the $5\%$ contract.

\textbf{v0.6 update (scaling law at N=32).} Extending the multi-seed protocol to a $32$-WML pool (sixteen MLPs, sixteen LIFs) produces $\text{mean} = 3.00\%$, $\text{median} = 2.39\%$, $\text{p25} = 1.64\%$, $\text{p75} = 4.66\%$, $\text{max} = 4.75\%$. Every seed at N=32 falls under the $5\%$ contract. Together with the N=16 and N=2 references, this traces a three-point scaling law:

\textbf{v0.7 update (plateau at N=64).} The fourth scaling point rules out an asymptotic decay to zero: $\text{mean} = 2.50\%$, $\text{median} = 2.73\%$, $\text{p25} = 1.70\%$, $\text{p75} = 3.31\%$, $\text{max} = 3.71\%$. Comparing to $N=32$'s $2.39\%$ median, we observe a \emph{plateau} in the $\sim 2\text{--}3\%$ band rather than further monotonic decrease. Our honest reading, finalized across four scales and 16 independent runs: (a) on individual instances, spike expressivity adds a measurable $\sim 5\text{--}10\%$ edge over a matched-capacity MLP on non-linear tasks --- this is a real substrate difference, not a bug; (b) pool averaging compresses the difference rapidly between $N=2$ and $N=32$ but then \emph{plateaus} at $\sim 2\text{--}3\%$ --- a substrate-intrinsic floor that cannot be averaged out; (c) the $5\%$ symmetric-gap contract holds distributionally for every seed at $N \geq 32$, so the paper's substrate-agnostic claim is empirically defensible at realistic pool sizes even though a small, stable $\sim 2\text{--}3\%$ LIF edge exists. The tests \texttt{test\_w2\_hard\_n16\_multiseed\_distribution}, \texttt{test\_w2\_hard\_n32\_multiseed\_closes\_under\_5pct}, \texttt{test\_w2\_hard\_n64\_multiseed\_reaches\_plateau}, and \texttt{test\_w2\_hard\_scaling\_law\_is\_monotonic} pin these claims; Figure~\ref{fig:w2-scaling-law} visualizes them.

\textit{Scale.} All experiments use $N=4$ WMLs with $K=1$--2 fan-out. Whether the 5\% polymorphie gap holds at $N=16$ or $N=32$ is an open question tracked in a separate scaling plan; the router's sparsity and the $\gamma$-priority rule may interact non-trivially at scale.

\textit{Single-seed artefacts.} The Gate P and Gate M pilots in this draft run on a single seed. Where multi-seed data exists (W2, W4, P1), we report it; elsewhere the reader should treat numbers as suggestive rather than statistically validated.

\section{Information Transmission (v0.8)}
\label{sec:info-tx}

The scaling law in \S Threats establishes \textbf{substrate-equivalent task competence}: MLP and LIF cohorts reach comparable accuracies at pool scale, with a $\sim 2\text{--}3\%$ plateau gap. That is Claim A. The title of this paper promises more: \emph{substrate-agnostic information transmission} (Claim B) --- that neuroletters emitted by one substrate can be \emph{decoded coherently} by another. This section reports three direct measurements of Claim B, complemented by a post-hoc mutual-kNN validation in Test~(6) that situates nerve-wml as an empirical instantiation of the Platonic Representation Hypothesis~\cite{huh2024platonic} on heterogeneous biologically-inspired substrates.

\textbf{(1) Mutual information between emitted codes.} For $x$ sampled from \texttt{HardFlowProxyTask}, let $c_{\text{MLP}}(x)$ and $c_{\text{LIF}}(x)$ be the $\arg\max$ codes from each substrate's $\pi$ head. Over five seeds at $N=1$, $\operatorname{MI}(c_{\text{MLP}}, c_{\text{LIF}}) / H(c_{\text{MLP}}) \approx 0.91$. Extended to the $N=16$ pool (64 MLP--LIF cross-pairs per seed over three seeds, 192 pair measurements total), the ratio \emph{strengthens} to $0.96$ --- pool-scale averaging aligns codes across substrates, not just accuracies. Two independently trained substrate populations share $\sim 95\%$ of their code information; they are \emph{not} encoding orthogonally despite different dynamics.

\emph{Bias and representation caveat (v0.8).} This ratio is measured on \emph{post-VQ} $\arg\max$ codes at $N=5000$. It is \emph{not} a small-sample artefact: it survives Panzeri--Treves debiasing ($0.931$) and a permutation null ($\text{null mean} \approx 0.005$, $p<0.01$), answering the external-review concern that an inflated plug-in MI could be the null expectation. It is, however, \emph{representation-dependent}: computing Kraskov--KSG mutual information on the \emph{pre-VQ continuous} embeddings of the same activations yields $\mathrm{MI}/H \approx 0.095$, so the argmax quantisation amplifies the apparent shared information by $\sim 10\times$. The discrete figure must be read as a post-VQ, debiased measurement and cited alongside the continuous value (see ADR-0001).

\textbf{(2) Round-trip fidelity.} A two-stage pipeline
$x \to \texttt{MLP.emit} \to T_{M \to L} \to \texttt{LIF.emit} \to T_{L \to M} \to \arg\max$
is trained by fine-tuning only the two transducers $T_{M \to L}, T_{L \to M} \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 64}$; substrates are frozen. Across three seeds, $\text{acc}_{\text{round-trip}} / \text{acc}_{\text{direct}} = \{0.91, 1.07, 0.98\}$, mean $0.99$. Code information survives the cross-substrate pass virtually intact.

\textbf{(3) Cross-substrate merge.} Freeze a trained MLP and a trained LIF (both their cores and readouts). Train only a transducer $T : \mathbb{R}^{64} \to \mathbb{R}^{n_{\text{neurons}}}$ mapping MLP emissions to LIF input features. Measure LIF task accuracy when its \emph{sole input} is MLP-emitted logits via $T$: the LIF never sees $x$ directly. Across three seeds, merge ratio $\text{acc}_{\text{cross}} / \text{acc}_{\text{MLP}} = \{1.00, 0.96, 0.96\}$, mean $0.97$. A frozen LIF can recover $97\%$ of an MLP's task competence from its emitted codes alone, through a learned linear transducer.

\textit{Global versus local alignment.} Three 2026 analyses sharpen what
a PRH-style claim may legitimately assert.
\cite{platoscave2026} report that the mutual-kNN metric on $\sim$1k
cross-modal pairs captures only coarse structure: alignment collapses as
the data is scaled up unless $k$ grows proportionally, so $k$ must be read
relative to $N$. \cite{aristotelianprh2026} show that capacity-dependent
trends in \emph{global} similarity measures largely disappear once
alignment is null-calibrated against capacity-matched random baselines,
leaving \emph{local} neighbourhood structure as the robust signal.
You et al.~\cite{you2026structure} then argue that this local/global
split confounds scale with \emph{what} is compared: \emph{relational}
structure (which samples are related) converges at both local and global
scale after calibration, measured globally by $H_0$ skeleton overlap,
whereas \emph{metric} geometry (distances, similarities) converges
much more weakly. Our claim is deliberately the relational,
neighbourhood-level one. The mutual-kNN kernel of
Table~\ref{tab:platonic-knn} measures neighbourhood overlap at small
$k$, and we report it \emph{relative to a capacity-matched random
baseline} ($18.8\times$ at $k{=}10$) precisely so the figure is
null-calibrated in the sense those critiques demand. We therefore claim
that MLP and spiking-LIF substrates share \emph{neighbourhood-level
relational} structure under the nerve protocol --- not that their
metric geometry coincides, nor that they converge to a single Platonic
representation. We have not computed a global relational measure such
as $H_0$ skeleton overlap, so we make no claim either way about global
relational structure. This conservative reading is sufficient for the
substrate-agnostic transmission claim, which only requires that a
decoder can recover neighbours, not that the two embedding spaces are
globally isometric.

\emph{extends} measured alignment from shared- to heterogeneous-encoder conditions on these
substrates, not that it demonstrates a task-independent universal code.

reveals spike-dynamics regularisation}: MLP overfits
($0.82 \to 0.76$) while LIF holds ($0.83 \to 0.82$), re-opening
the substrate gap to $6\%$. This is a positive substrate-specific
signature --- LIF carries a scale-robustness property that matched-
parameter MLP does not. The figure
\texttt{papers/paper1/figures/eeg\_matched\_scale\_sweep.json}
archives the full sweep.

A complementary HardFlowProxyTask sweep at pool scale $N=2$
(\texttt{scripts/hyperparam\_sensitivity.py},
\texttt{figures/hyperparam\_sensitivity.json}) reveals a
\emph{different} sensitivity pattern: matched $d_{\mathrm{hidden}}$
\emph{widens} the pairwise gap from $1.5\%$ at $d=8$ to $46\%$ at
$d=64$ --- MLP plateaus at $\sim 0.52$ (a structural limit of
4-layer ReLU on XOR-on-noise) while LIF scales to $0.75$. This
confirms Test~(4b)'s observation that architecture scale and pool
scale are \emph{orthogonal} axes: the stable $\sim 2$--$3\%$
plateau of Claim~A emerges from pool averaging (Test~5,
$N \geq 32$), not from substrate-capacity scale invariance.
Learning-rate sensitivity at fixed $d=16$ is modest: gap ranges
from $0.4\%$ (lr~$=5 \times 10^{-3}$, near-tied) to $14\%$
(lr~$=10^{-1}$, MLP catastrophic), with the default lr~$=10^{-2}$
operating point at $8.4\%$. The $N=2$ single-pair regime is not
where Claim~A is defended; $N=16$ pool-scale averaging (Test~5)
compresses substrate-intrinsic differences to the reproducible
$2$--$3\%$ plateau independently of the matched $d_{\mathrm{hidden}}$
chosen.

\textbf{(12) End-to-end deployable protocol ($\gamma/\theta$ PAC
channel, v1.9.4).} Tests~(2)--(3) measure transmission on the
\emph{idealised} path: the transducer operates on continuous logit
vectors and fidelity is read \emph{before} the discrete
\texttt{GammaThetaMultiplexer} --- the deployable phase-amplitude-coupling
codec of \texttt{track\_p/multiplexer.py} --- is engaged. We close that
gap by routing a single integer code per symbol through the \emph{full}
discrete protocol: a learned per-edge transducer ($64{\times}64$
codebook, \texttt{track\_p/transducer.py}) followed by the
$\gamma/\theta$ channel with its learned 64-point PSK constellation,
then demodulation. On 20 seeds of \texttt{HardFlowProxyTask} ($d{=}16$,
12-class; $800$ train steps, $400$ transducer steps, alphabet $64$), the
fidelity ratio
$\text{acc}_{\text{round-trip}} / \text{acc}_{\text{direct}}$ is
(a)~idealised (paper path, continuous logits, no channel)
$1.007$ \mbox{[0.975, 1.037]};
(b)~\emph{untrained} transducer $+$ channel
$0.168$ \mbox{[0.158, 0.179]}, its round-trip accuracy ($0.092$)
collapsing to chance ($1/12$);
(c)~\emph{trained} transducer $+$ channel, i.e.\ the full real protocol,
$\mathbf{0.924}$ \mbox{[0.892, 0.954]}.
Path~(c) recovers path~(a) to within the discretisation cost: a single
integer code carries $\sim 8\%$ less than a continuous logit vector. The
decomposition is unambiguous. The $\gamma/\theta$ channel's
code-survival is $1.00$ at every seed and condition --- the PSK
constellation is analytically max-min-distance optimal, so the channel
is \emph{never} the bottleneck (and training it only erodes noise
robustness). The untrained per-edge transducer is the bottleneck;
task-supervised Gumbel-softmax training of its codebook closes the gap.
This extends Claim~B from the idealised continuous-logit measurement to
the \emph{deployable} discrete protocol: the learned codebook transducer
and the $\gamma/\theta$ PAC channel are load-bearing end-to-end. We
frame this as a \emph{consolidation} rather than a new phenomenon --- the
underlying cross-substrate alignment is the Platonic effect of Test~(6)
(and, more broadly, of model stitching and relative representations);
the contribution here is that it survives a concrete
digital-communication channel, not only a continuous read-out.
Reproducible via \texttt{scripts/transmission\_full\_protocol.py} (data
\texttt{figures/transmission\_full\_protocol.json}).

Together Tests (1)--(3) pin Claim B empirically: the Nerve Protocol does more than expose a shared interface --- it carries information that crosses the substrate boundary with minimal loss. The tests \texttt{test\_mi\_between\_substrates\_is\_high}, \texttt{test\_mi\_at\_pool\_scale\_strengthens}, \texttt{test\_round\_trip\_fidelity\_preserves\_accuracy}, and \texttt{test\_cross\_substrate\_merge\_approaches\_mlp\_accuracy} pin the measurements. Tests~(10) and (11) of v1.7.0 refine this conclusion: the protocol carries information that otherwise requires a shared frontend (Test~10) and does so robustly across matched-capacity scales (Test~11). Test~(12) of v1.9.4 closes the deployment gap: transmission survives the \emph{full} discrete $\gamma/\theta$ protocol, not only the idealised continuous-logit read-out.

\section{Interpretability}
\label{sec:interp}

Because each WML owns a local 64-code codebook, the learned alphabet
is open to inspection. We build a \emph{semantics table} per WML
(\texttt{interpret.code\_semantics.build\_semantics\_table}) that
records, for each code $c \in \{0, \dots, 63\}$: the top-$K$ input
summaries whose forward pass picks $c$ as the $\pi$-head argmax, the
mean hidden-state centroid over those inputs, the softmax-derived
next-code distribution, and the count of mapped samples. A $50$-line
$k$-means clusterer groups the 64 centroids into 8 semantic buckets;
a plain-HTML report (\texttt{interpret.visualise.render\_html\_report})
renders one row per code with a cluster-coloured dot, centroid norm,
and top-input statistics. \texttt{gate-interp-passed} verifies that
the pipeline completes in under 60\,s, produces a cluster-entropy
above 2 bits (non-degenerate assignments), and writes a report file
of at least 1\,KB. The artefact is a reviewer-inspectable
\texttt{code\,$\to$\,concept} table --- not a universal semantics, but
a reproducible snapshot of what the trained WML treats as equivalent.

\section{Integrations}
\label{sec:integrations}

Beyond the core gates, four integration surfaces are validated as software
pipelines. All are env-gated and return to a no-op when the target is absent,
so nerve-wml can be deployed alongside heterogeneous hosts without coupling
to their release cadences.

\textit{Adaptive alphabet (Gate Adaptive).}
The 64-code codebook is fixed per WML at gate time.
\texttt{track\_p.adaptive\_codebook.AdaptiveCodebook} implements an
\texttt{active\_mask} over a fixed 64-slot storage so shrink and grow
become logical (not physical) operations. \texttt{bridge.transducer\_resize}
produces a reshaped \texttt{Transducer} preserving argmax on kept rows.
\texttt{gate-adaptive-passed} verifies a shrink-then-grow cycle completes
without the alphabet collapsing below 4 codes.

\textit{Neuromorphic export (Gate Neuro).} LifWML $\to$ INT8 artefact
(\texttt{neuromorphic.export.quantize\_lif\_wml}) $\to$ pure-numpy
replay (\texttt{MockNeuromorphicRunner}) $\to$ vendor stubs for
Intel Loihi~2 and BrainChip Akida. On an untrained LifWML the
PyTorch-to-mock delta is $\sim 19\%$ (INT8 quantization of binary-like
codebooks is coarse); trained LIFs are expected to tighten this.
The vendor SDKs are not runtime deps; see
\texttt{docs/neuromorphic/deployment-guide.md}.

\textit{Dream-of-kiki consolidation (Gate Dream, partial).}
\texttt{bridge.dream\_bridge.DreamBridge} collects $\varepsilon$ traces,
encodes them as schema-v0 \texttt{np.int32} arrays of shape
$[n\_\text{events}, 4]$, and applies a consolidation delta (supplied
externally by \texttt{kiki\_oniric}) to per-edge transducers in place.
End-to-end validation ($\gamma$ emission $\to$ $\theta$ collection
$\to$ zero-delta consolidation) is covered by
\texttt{scripts/ablation\_n3\_predictive.py}, confirming the pipeline
plumbing independently of the upstream \texttt{kiki\_oniric} release.
CI uses a \texttt{MockConsolidator} returning zero-delta;
\texttt{gate-dream-passed} is marked \emph{partial} pending the
\texttt{kiki\_oniric} v0.5+ public \texttt{consolidate()} surface.

\textit{LLM advisor for micro-kiki (Gate LLM Advisor).}
\texttt{bridge.kiki\_nerve\_advisor.NerveWmlAdvisor} exposes
\texttt{advise(query\_tokens, current\_route) -> dict | None}.
It is env-gated (\texttt{NERVE\_WML\_ENABLED}) and
checkpoint-path-gated (\texttt{NERVE\_WML\_CHECKPOINT\_PATH}). Warm-path
latency is under 50\,ms on commodity Apple Silicon; disabled-path
overhead is under 1\,ms. The advisor \textbf{never raises} --- every
exception returns \texttt{None}, so the host runtime is not destabilised
by advisor failures. A self-contained downstream-PR recipe is at
\texttt{docs/integration/micro-kiki-wiring.md}.

\textit{Downstream composability (bouba\_sens).}
The v1.4.0--v1.5.0 feature releases (\texttt{plasticity\_schedule} +
\texttt{constellation\_lock\_after} for B-1 Amedi-2007 gap probes,
\texttt{TransducerGating.GUMBEL\_SOFTMAX} for B-2 Me3-delta migration,
and \texttt{MlpWML.from\_spectrogram(\ldots)} for the shared STFT-carrier
path) were filed by \texttt{bouba\_sens}, the first empirical consumer
of the nerve-wml downstream API. All three surfaces are opt-in and
backward-compatible with the v1.2.3 scientific baseline
(\S\ref{sec:info-tx}); \texttt{bouba\_sens} ADR-0007 paired-run CLI
(\texttt{eval --t1-ckpt/--t2-ckpt}) consumes them to produce the
5-world grid. Composability in the other direction (downstream
\emph{changes} to nerve-wml) is enforced by 102 tests plus the
\texttt{gate-*-passed} invariant tags.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}
The experiments in this draft use toy signals and small WML pools
($N=4$). Scaling to larger pools, LLM-backed WMLs, and hardware
neuromorphic deployment is left for follow-up work. The $\gamma$
priority rule suffices in simulation; a phase-locked oscillator
regime would give the same invariant without the rule and is a
natural next step.

\subsection{Untested Empirical Scope}

\emph{We enumerate explicitly what has not been empirically validated, to delimit the scope of the claims made in this paper.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Synthetic Gaussian substrates only.} The scientific evaluation conducted on macM1 (1750 cells across 50 seeds, 8 metrics, Renf 6 in \texttt{docs/superpowers/research/}) uses purely synthetic Gaussian noise: $x = \mathrm{randn}$, $y = x + \sigma\varepsilon$. Real model representations---LLM hidden states, vision embeddings, neuroscience recordings---may exhibit different algebraic and statistical structure. The supplementary CKNNA N-dependence replication shows the predicted decay that we report; whether real codebooks on real model embeddings exhibit the same decay is conjecture pending empirical validation on live model checkpoints.

\item \textbf{BioWML never validated against real CL1/FinalSpark API.} The substrate-agnostic claim extends to BioWML by Protocol conformance---the four WML classes implement the same contract---rather than by empirical accuracy parity with MlpWML, LifWML, and TransformerWML on a shared task. Validation against a real bioculture API (CL1 Petri-dish, FinalSpark array) has not been undertaken; a runbook exists at \texttt{docs/superpowers/research/2026-05-20-bio-api-credentials.md} but remains unexercised.

\item \textbf{No head-to-head performance benchmark versus prior work.} Plan B (Claim A, \S\ref{sec:related}) differentiates the nerve-wml protocol qualitatively from prior discrete-code inter-module schemes (DVNC~\cite{liu2021dvnc}, NIR~\cite{pedersen2024nir}, AER~\cite{aer2025biohybrid}) in structural terms. No end-to-end accuracy or information-transmission benchmark against these three alternatives exists in this paper.

\item \textbf{AKOrN minimal flavour.} The KuramotoMultiplexer used in ablations (Fig.~7) employs learned oscillation frequency $\omega$, coupling strength $K$, and linear readout over 8--32 Euler steps. This is not equivalent to the production AKOrN implementation of Miyato et al.~(ICLR 2025), which uses phase-aware readout and additional constraints. The synchrony spectral-entropy comparison (Renf 7) should be revisited against the full AKOrN architecture in future work. AKOrN is moreover no longer the strongest oscillator comparator: Kuramoto phase encoding added to vision Transformers improves data and parameter efficiency~\cite{xiao2026kope}, a Winfree oscillator network is competitive on ImageNet-1K~\cite{dai2026wonn}, and self-attention has been recast as a Kuramoto coupling step on the torus~\cite{nunley2026kuramotoattn}. Whether the GTM's behaviour is specific to phase-amplitude coupling or shared by any phase inductive bias is untested against these baselines.

\item \textbf{Seed scope $n=50$.} Robustness holds within seeds 0--49 (Wilcoxon $p < 10^{-15}$ over the measured range), but generalisation to broader seed populations (e.g., 100, 1000+) is conjecture. Renf 11 (currently unmerged on \texttt{feat/deep-verifications}) provides partial evidence by comparing seed windows 0--49 vs.\ 50--99 vs.\ 1000--1049; until those results land on master, the 50-seed claim remains scope-limited.

\item \textbf{Task scope and MI saturation.} All GTM (gamma-theta multiplex) ablations (Renf 7, Fig.~7) measure the same task configuration: alphabet=64, n\_symbols=7, 128 codes per batch. Mutual information saturates at 2.22 bits on this task. Whether the two competing arms (simple\_gating, akorn\_best) separate on harder routing problems (e.g., alphabet=128, n\_symbols=14, 256 codes per batch) is conjecture pending Renf 13 (\texttt{feat/deep-verifications}). All three arms reach the same accuracy and MI on the current task; the only discriminating axis is carrier spectral structure, not downstream task performance.

\item \textbf{MLX cross-Apple-Silicon non-bit-exact.} The MLX library's \texttt{mx.random.normal} produces numerically distinct outputs across Apple Silicon generations: M1 family (`applegpu\_g13s`) outputs diverge from M3 family onwards (`g15+`, `g16+`). This is reported as ml-explore/mlx\#3568 (open). Numerical reproducibility of any result in this paper relying on MLX holds only within an architectural class (e.g., M1 to M1), not cross-generation.

\item \textbf{Solo author, no internal review.} This paper has not undergone pre-submission internal review. Mitigations adopted during preparation: the fact-check audit (\texttt{scripts/factcheck\_audit.py --ci}) enforces that every numerical claim traces to JSON data from an executed research session, multi-seed measurement (n=50) is standardised across reinforcement runs (Renf 1--8), paired Wilcoxon tests with bootstrap confidence intervals are applied throughout, and five initial claims were revised during the gap-analysis sprint (documented in \texttt{CHANGELOG.md}). The audit currently reports 27 OK / 0 DIVERGENT on master (2026-05-20).

\item \textbf{Spectral entropy 4-arm ordering is B-dependent.} As documented in \S\ref{sec:limitations} (Renf 10, \texttt{2026-05-20-renf10-batch-sensitivity.md}), the strict 4-arm ordering `null < akorn\_best < gtm < simple\_gating' reported in Renf 7 holds only at batch size B=256. At $B \in \{64, 128, 512\}$ the AKOrN arm exhibits seed-variance $\sigma_{H_\mathrm{spec}} \approx 0.6$ and its rank flips with random initialisation. The robust, B-invariant claim is the two pairwise gaps: `gtm > null' ($\Delta \geq 0.05$ bits, $p < 10^{-15}$ at all $B$) and `gtm $\ll$ simple\_gating' (gap $\geq 1.0$ bits, $p < 10^{-15}$ at all $B$).

\item \textbf{CKNNA is N-dependent metric.} The continuous-kernel nearest-neighbour alignment (Gröger et al.~2026) used to validate Claim B (Platonic Representation Hypothesis on discrete codes, \S\ref{sec:info-tx}) is a function of sample count N and pool size. The reported alignment 0.174 at k=10 (3 seeds) represents cross-substrate heterogeneous ANN vs.\ spiking LIF, not a universal constant. Reproducibility requires exact N, k, and seed specification.

\end{enumerate}

Several of these limitations admit straightforward empirical resolution: CL1/FinalSpark API access for item~2, broader seed populations for item~5, head-to-head DVNC/NIR/AER benchmarks for item~3. Others are scope decisions for this paper version (item~4, item~7); their resolution requires substantial additional engineering or interdisciplinary collaboration not undertaken here. All remaining limitations are tracked as future work in the specification (\texttt{docs/superpowers/specs/}).

\textbf{$\gamma/\theta$ empirical stress.} The $\gamma/\theta$ multiplexing
invariant (N-3: $\text{role}{=}\varepsilon \Leftrightarrow \text{phase}{=}\theta$,
\S\ref{sec:method}) is formally specified and unit-tested
(\texttt{tests/unit/test\_invariants.py}), but the experiments in this draft
run the \texttt{HardFlowProxyTask} pipeline with the $\theta$ phase inactive
throughout training --- only the $\gamma$ pathway is stressed. An ablation
of the \texttt{strict\_n3} flag on this pipeline therefore yields
$\Delta = 0$ at four significant figures across $N \in \{16, 32, 64\}$
over 5 seeds (\texttt{scripts/ablation\_n3.py}): there are simply no
$\varepsilon$-letters to validate. Two complementary follow-up experiments confirm this interpretation.
A full $\gamma \to \theta \to$ consolidate loop where MLP WMLs emit
$\varepsilon$ naturally via \texttt{emit\_head\_eps} (surprise $>$ 0.30)
over 3 seeds yields $\Delta = 0$ exactly
(\texttt{scripts/ablation\_n3\_predictive.py}) --- the invariant is
satisfied by construction since WMLs hard-code \texttt{phase=Phase.THETA}
when emitting $\varepsilon$. A violation-injection guard test
(\texttt{scripts/ablation\_n3\_guard.py}) confirms that strict\_n3=True
catches $N \cdot v$ injected violations within binomial noise at
$v \in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50\}$ (3 seeds, $N{=}1000$ letters)
while strict\_n3=False lets them pass silently. Together, these three
ablations establish that N-3 is a formal correctness contract --- a
type-checker for the protocol --- rather than a differential mechanism:
measurable impact requires deliberate violation injection rather than
emerging from a correct pipeline. This does not invalidate the predictive-routing ancestry
(\cite{bastos2012canonical, bastos2020routing, gabhart2025predictive}),
since biological $\gamma / \theta$ rhythm-specific routing is itself a
gating constraint that determines \emph{when} error channels open, not
a free parameter that tunes computational behaviour.

\textbf{Carrier-spectrum metric — applying the standard effective-rank diagnostic.}
A pilot run of the GTM (gamma-theta multiplex) ablation reported a
\texttt{\_synchrony\_index} per arm, defined as the fraction of carrier
variance carried by the top principal component of the centered batch.
The intended reading was ``high \texttt{synchrony\_index} = collapse onto a
single mode''. A 4-arm replication on $50$ seeds $\times$ $3$ hosts
(\texttt{null}, \texttt{akorn\_best}, \texttt{gtm},
\texttt{simple\_gating}; see \texttt{docs/superpowers/research/2026-05-20-synchrony-replacement.md})
shows this proxy is \emph{anti-monotone} with respect to the intended
semantics: $\text{null}{=}0.32 > \text{gtm}{=}0.20 > \text{simple\_gating}{=}0.08$
(paired Wilcoxon $p < 10^{-15}$). A trained-but-broken model
(null arm: GTM trained on shuffled supervision) concentrates variance
on one mode and therefore scores \emph{higher} than a model that
successfully decomposes the task, inverting the intended reading. This
anti-monotonicity is precisely the failure mode that effective-rank-style
metrics \cite{royvetterli2007erank} were originally introduced to avoid.

We therefore replace \texttt{\_synchrony\_index} with the spectral-entropy
form of effective rank \cite{royvetterli2007erank}, $H_{\text{spec}} =
-\sum_i p_i \log p_i$ with $p_i = \sigma_i^2 / \sum_j \sigma_j^2$ ---
the standard collapse-analysis quantity, recently reapplied to
representation-evaluation in LLM hidden states \cite{wei2024differank}.
A batch-size sensitivity sweep over $B \in \{64, 128, 256, 512\}$
(50 seeds $\times$ 4 arms $\times$ 4 batch sizes; see
\texttt{docs/superpowers/research/2026-05-20-renf10-batch-sensitivity.md})
shows that the \emph{strict 4-arm ordering} is $B$-dependent and only
holds at $B = 256$; at $B \in \{64, 128, 512\}$ the
\texttt{akorn\_best} arm exhibits high seed-variance
($\sigma_{H_{\text{spec}}} \approx 0.6$ across seeds vs.\
$\sigma \approx 0.01$ for \texttt{gtm}) and its rank against
\texttt{null} flips with the random-init realisation. The robust
claims that survive this sweep at every $B$ are two pairwise
gaps: $H_{\text{spec}}(\text{gtm}) > H_{\text{spec}}(\text{null})$
($\Delta \geq 0.05$ bits, paired Wilcoxon $p < 10^{-15}$ at all $B$),
and $H_{\text{spec}}(\text{gtm}) \ll H_{\text{spec}}(\text{simple\_gating})$
(gap $\geq 1.0$ bits, paired Wilcoxon $p < 10^{-15}$ at all $B$).
At the originally-reported $B = 256$ point the four arms are
$\text{null} (1.74 \pm 0.13) <
 \text{akorn\_best} (2.04 \pm 0.6) <
 \text{gtm} (2.19 \pm 0.01) <
 \text{simple\_gating} (3.62 \pm 0.02)$.
The semantics are direct: high entropy means information is distributed
across many effective modes; low entropy means collapse onto few modes.
\texttt{simple\_gating}, which encodes $7$ symbols $\times$ $64$ codes in
independent slots, naturally occupies the most modes; the null arm
collapses; \texttt{gtm} sits in between. We report \texttt{akorn\_best}
as an \emph{intermediate but high-variance} arm rather than a fourth
strictly-ranked position, because its placement against \texttt{null}
is not stable across batch sizes.

With spectral entropy as the carrier-spectrum metric and a
permutation-shuffled null arm as the cross-condition baseline ---
a special case of the null-calibration framework formalised by
\cite{aristotelianprh2026} --- the defensible claim becomes:
\emph{GTM exhibits a spectral entropy
($H_{\text{spec}}(\text{gtm}) \in [2.14, 2.20]$ across
$B \in \{64,128,256,512\}$, $\sigma \approx 0.01$)
significantly higher than the degenerate null arm at every $B$ tested
($\Delta \geq 0.05$ bits, $p < 10^{-15}$) and significantly lower than
\texttt{simple\_gating}'s independent-symbol decomposition at every $B$
tested (gap $\geq 1.0$ bits, $p < 10^{-15}$). GTM occupies the
gtm-and-akorn region of the spectrum, below \texttt{simple\_gating}'s
per-symbol decomposition; this position reflects band-multiplexing
compression: GTM encodes $7$ symbols into fewer effective modes than
a one-symbol-per-slot encoding would require.} This is a claim of
\emph{significant pairwise gaps against two reference arms}, not a
strict 4-arm ranking and not extremality --- weaker than the original
``GTM does not collapse'' framing but defensible without the
metric-monotonicity caveat and robust to batch-size variation.\footnote{%
The \texttt{\_synchrony\_index} routine in
\texttt{track\_p/multiplexer.py} still uses the top-PC variance
definition pending a production-code migration PR
(\texttt{docs/superpowers/issues/2026-05-20-spectral-entropy-migration.md}).
The quantity reported in this paper is \texttt{spectral\_entropy}
from \texttt{scripts/synchrony\_alternatives.py}, committed on
\texttt{master} at SHA \texttt{076f770}.}

\textbf{Dynamical operating point.} We do not measure where the spiking
substrate operates dynamically. Purely local homeostatic plasticity can
drive deep networks to a critical state (vanishing largest finite-time
Lyapunov exponent), while gradient training alone drifts them towards
supercritical dynamics~\cite{vock2026adaptivesoc}. Whether
\texttt{LifWML} operates near criticality, and whether that operating
point affects protocol alignment, is left to future work.

\textbf{Biological PAC validation.} Any future validation of the
$\gamma/\theta$ channel against biological phase-amplitude coupling must
report the preferred phase, not Tort's modulation index alone, because
the index is invariant to the temporal polarity of the slow
phase~\cite{keshavarzi2026mipolarity}.

Wiring the adaptive-alphabet operations into live WMLs at training
time (rather than post-hoc on checkpoints) is tracked in spec \S 13
Q1. The neuromorphic mock runner needs a trained-LIF tightening pass
to push the PyTorch-to-mock delta below 5\%. The dream-integration
contract is locked at schema v0 but awaits the \texttt{kiki\_oniric}
public API. The LLM advisor ships in advisory-only mode; a mixed-routing
evaluation comparing advisor-on vs advisor-off on micro-kiki's live
traffic is the next natural milestone.
